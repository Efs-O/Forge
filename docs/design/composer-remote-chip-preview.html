D · what drives it

The state already exists host-side. Nothing new needs to be measured or stored — it needs an event and a bridge message.

FactOwner todayMeans
transport running RemoteRuntime.active — a Map keyed telegram/whatsapp, written only by startTransport/stopTransport/dispose Forge started the bot and channel.start() resolved
owner paired RemoteAuth.hasOwner(channel) — reads forge.remote.<ch>.ownerId from SecretStorage a person is bound to this transport and can actually send
Why two states and not one

"Remote active" on a transport with no paired owner would be false. The bot is up and answering /pair, but nobody can drive Forge through it — that is the whole point of the pairing gate. Showing the same blue chip for both would mean the indicator says "someone can reach this window" when nobody can. Hence state 4, grey and unpulsed. If you would rather have one state, say so and I ship only the blue one.

What the chip cannot honestly claim

It reports Forge's own view: the transport started and an owner is paired. It is not a health check. A revoked Telegram token, a dropped long-poll, or a WhatsApp session that Baileys has quietly lost will all still read as active until a send fails. Making the chip mean "the link is up right now" needs a heartbeat that does not exist yet, and I would rather it under-claim than invent one.

Both transports at once

Telegram and WhatsApp can both be live. One chip, not two: it reads Remote active either way and names the transports in its tooltip (telegram · whatsapp). Two chips would push the centred model off-axis on a narrow panel to say something the tooltip already says.

Cost

An emitter on RemoteRuntime fired from the three sites that mutate active, plus a remoteStatus bridge message, plus the chip and a three-column grid in #input-actions. No new state, no polling, no network.
